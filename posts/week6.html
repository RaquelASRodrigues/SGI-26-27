When words
choose a team.

Can we recognize a link-defined Marvel community from the language of its character pages—and where do words and structure disagree?

One question, two maps.

Network communities tell us which pages are densely linked. Their prose might tell a different story: shared settings, publishing history, or a handful of famous names can travel across a boundary. We wanted to make that disagreement inspectable rather than treat a classifier score as a verdict.

We took the largest linked communities and represented each page as TF–IDF word weights. Stopwords stay in: rare, community-specific words earn their influence through IDF.

Loading the recorded preprocessing choices…

Ask the held-out page.

For each character, we hid its page from the model, trained on the other pages, and asked which community it belonged to. That cross-validation detail matters: without it, a score can partly be a memory test. The switch below repeats the experiment after removing detected names, so we can ask how much of the answer is story rather than surnames and team labels.

What this does not prove.

Loading scope…

A prediction is not a discovered essence of a community, and a misfit is not an error in the network. Communities here were themselves made from links on these same Wikipedia pages; their language also reflects how editors write, what publication history gets repeated, and which material has been documented. This is a useful comparison of two representations, not proof of fictional affiliation or causation.
