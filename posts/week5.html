How much of Marvel's Wikipedia is copied from itself, and is it prose or scaffolding?

A web of repeats.

Search by description.

Asked. Can a plain bag-of-words search find a character from a short description? Did. Lowercased Unicode-letter tokens become count vectors; rank pages by cosine similarity, with an optional English stopword filter.

Search data loads when you start typing.

    Saw. The search can return semantically nearby pages, but it can also rank by incidental word overlap. Checked in the text. The corpus slice has no plain Thor or main Iron Man node; it contains Thor Girl and Iron Man variants instead. Limitation. Cosine over word counts cannot infer synonyms, entity identity, or whether a query is answerable from these character pages.

    The browser tokenizer is /\p{L}+(?:['’]\p{L}+)*/gu, lowercased, with punctuation and number-only tokens dropped. Its Python counterpart uses Unicode letter categories. The build exports Python-tokenized fixtures and checks them against the browser tokenizer.

    What surprised me: the search failure is often structural, not algorithmic. A query for Norse thunder-god Thor cannot return a plain Thor page that is absent from this graph slice.

    Words, links, and length.

    Asked. Do text mentions align with directed links, how does vocabulary accumulate, and do more connected characters have longer pages? Did. Matched target-page title strings (including undisambiguated forms) in source pages, fitted Heaps' law over seeded random page orders, and compared undirected degree with token length.

    Saw. Many directed edges have a literal target-title mention; vocabulary growth follows a strong log-log fit, and degree correlates with article length. Checked in the text. Spider-Man has the highest degree in this slice, while Scarlet Witch has its longest page. Limitation. A name mention is not proof of a semantic relation; degree is only a visibility proxy, and correlation does not show that editors expand articles because characters are more connected in fiction.

    What surprised me: the degree–length correlation is strong, but Scarlet Witch's page is longer than Spider-Man's despite much lower degree—connectivity and page length are related, not interchangeable.

    A machine dreams in teams.

    Asked. Can a community-specific trigram model write a convincing character page? Did. Trained one model for each of the five largest communities, backed off trigram → bigram → unigram with a fixed multiplier per backoff, and generated fixed-seed samples after replacing detected character names and aliases with a neutral placeholder.

    Loading generated sample…
    Negative result. We expected plausible fragments; the generated passages remain unreadable mixtures of biographies, reviews, issue listings, and plots even with punctuation retained and names masked.

    Saw. The name-free centroid baseline exceeds chance, but the text does not read like a page. Checked in the text. All generated samples were read; residual phrases such as section headings and issue titles remain. Limitation. The centroid uses the same community corpus that trained the generator, so this is not a held-out classification result; the alias mask is also not a full entity recognizer.

    What surprised me: without grammar or syntax structures, the model stayed unreadable. In the unmasked run, character-name leakage alone made community lookup trivial.

    A weirdness leaderboard.

    Asked. Which page looks most unlike its length peers? Did. Ranked TTR, non-alphabetic share, zlib ratio, and distance from the corpus centroid within equal-count page-length bins, then averaged percentile ranks.

    RankPageTokensAverage rank

    Saw. Shamrock, Guardsman, and Super Rabbit rank highly. Checked in the text. Shamrock's luck/poltergeist premise and Super Rabbit's talking-animal premise are genuine content; Super Rabbit also has dates, issue numbers, and currency symbols that affect the measures. Limitation. Decile controls reduce length effects but do not remove small-sample effects; this is a heuristic ranking, not an objective weirdness score.

    What surprised me: Super Rabbit ranks highly partly through real subject-matter distinctiveness and partly through formatting-heavy publication history.
