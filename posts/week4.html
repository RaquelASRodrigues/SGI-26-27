Is the observed modularity unusual under rewiring?

A directed degree-preserving null model rewires arc endpoints while preserving every philosopher’s in- and out-degree and the directed arc count. The aggregated edge-weight values are shuffled across rewired arcs, so the global weight distribution and total weight are retained, but node strengths and weight-to-endpoint pairings are not. This tests structure against this specific null; it does not establish that detected groups are philosophical schools.

    Follow Aristotle’s actual Wikipedia-article links. Each directed line is coloured by its computed link community; hover to inspect the source, target, direction, weight and assignment. Click a neighbour—or choose someone in a selected group—to follow their own connections.

    What “community” means here: link communities compare links by the Jaccard similarity of the inclusive neighbourhoods of their endpoints, hierarchically cluster similar links, then select a cut with maximum partition density. Communities are groups of similar links, not exclusive sets of philosophers: a philosopher can inherit several memberships when their incident links fall into different groups. IDs are algorithmic structural labels, not verified philosophical traditions; the network records article relationships, not similarity of philosophical ideas.

    The link method symmetrizes the directed graph by collapsing reciprocal source–target pairs into one undirected connection and summing their weights in that projection. Similarity uses binary neighbourhoods, so the summed weights do not affect clustering. The visualization retains each original directed arc and weight, colouring it with the computed community of its undirected pair.

    An individual k-clique is exactly k philosophers with a connection between every pair. K-clique percolation joins such cliques when they share k−1 philosophers, so a resulting community can contain far more than k nodes and can overlap another community. The comparison precomputes k=3, 4 and 5 on a binary undirected projection: a pair is connected if at least one directed article link exists either way; reciprocal links collapse, weights are ignored, and the source has no self-loops. K-clique results assign memberships to nodes, not edges.

    What survives a backbone filter?.

    A statistical backbone asks which weighted connections stand out relative to a network null model—not which links are “important” in every historical sense. The disparity filter tests each directed edge against the source’s outgoing and target’s incoming weight distributions.

    Parameter α is a significance threshold. For each endpoint, the filter compares an edge’s share of that endpoint’s total strength with a uniform allocation across its distinct neighbors. It keeps the arc if its disparity tail probability is at most α at either endpoint (the OR rule). Direction is preserved; incoming and outgoing tests are separate. Repeated records for the same directed pair are combined by summing their weights. A smaller α is stricter.

    Both views use the same α. The left view keeps every original directed, weighted arc; the right keeps only arcs that pass the disparity filter. Counts are directed arcs, not unique neighbours—reciprocal arcs count separately. Line thickness always represents the original weight; direction colour and retention styling are independent.

    Do connection weights change which philosophers belong together?

    The philosopher edge file records a directed link when one source article links to another target article. Its weight is the number of such links; duplicate directed source–target rows are summed before analysis. It counts article links, not philosophical agreement, relationship strength, or importance.

    This is a controlled comparison: the unweighted representation gives every distinct directed arc weight 1, while the weighted representation keeps its aggregated data weight. Both use the same philosophers, including isolates, the same directed arcs, the same layout, and the same community algorithm settings within each comparison.

    When this option is checked, changed philosophers remain prominent; unchanged nodes and unrelated connections are retained faintly as spatial context. Magenta rings mark the changed set used by both this view and the count.

    NMI compares each algorithm’s weighted and unweighted partitions on the same 1,444 philosophers: closer to 1 means greater agreement, closer to 0 means lower agreement; it does not say which partition is correct. Community IDs are arbitrary labels. The changed count compares each philosopher’s co-members, not its numeric ID; all isolates are retained as singleton communities in both Infomap runs and included in NMI.
