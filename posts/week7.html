Similar stories,
distant pages.

Are Marvel characters whose Wikipedia descriptions are similar also close in the hyperlink network—or do words and links tell different stories?

Two ways to be close.

A page can describe characters with similar powers, teams, or story arcs without linking to them. Conversely, a page can link to another character for a brief plot connection that leaves little shared language. Comparing text with links lets us see where these two kinds of relationship agree—and where the encyclopedia’s prose and its navigation diverge.

We use the 303 character pages supplied for this project and the Week 1 directed link network. Text neighbours are every other page whose cosine similarity meets the selected threshold. Hyperlink neighbours are pages joined to the selected character by a single direct edge; incoming links point to the selected page, outgoing links leave it. A multi-step path does not count as a hyperlink neighbour.

TF-IDF cosine. TF-IDF weights a page’s words by how frequent they are on that page and how uncommon they are across the corpus; cosine similarity compares the resulting vectors. It is fast and reproducible, but mostly captures shared vocabulary—not meaning expressed with different words. Stopwords are removed, term frequency is sublinear, and vectors are L2-normalized.
Neural embedding cosine. The locally run sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model encodes context into 384-dimensional vectors. We mean-pool its last-layer content-token representations and L2-normalize each page vector before cosine comparison. The default 256-token model input limit is handled by splitting every full page into non-overlapping chunks of at most 254 content tokens; all chunk token representations contribute, weighted by the number of content tokens. No page is silently truncated. This captures contextual similarities as well as shared wording, but chunk boundaries lose some long-range context.

Both methods use the same full text with whitespace collapsed. TF-IDF applies its documented stop-word and weighting choices; embeddings retain the model tokenizer’s full token stream. Their scores have different scales and must be interpreted within their own method, not compared numerically.

A fixed-network null test

Loading the permutation test…

The null keeps the hyperlink graph and its directed edges fixed, then randomly reassigns the page text representations to nodes. Every run scores the same 1,784 directed edges. This asks whether the observed edge endpoints tend to carry more similar TF-IDF representations than random text-to-node assignment would produce. It does not show that shared wording caused links, control for page length, degree or editorial attention, or establish agreement for embedding semantics.

What the score can’t say.

TF-IDF is sensitive to repeated names, team labels, and formulaic article language. Cosine values depend on this corpus and preprocessing; a TF-IDF score should not be compared numerically with an embedding score from a different model. Page lengths and editorial coverage vary, and links reflect article conventions as well as character relationships. The text set also contains disconnected and isolated pages: they still receive text rankings, but have no direct hyperlink neighbours in the corresponding direction.

In this dataset, text similarity and direct links are related on average, yet they are not interchangeable. Some characters share nearly all of their top-ranked text neighbours with their direct links, while other high-ranking text pairs have no direct edge and some actual links connect pages with little shared vocabulary. The explorer makes those differences inspectable character by character; it does not turn either kind of similarity into a definitive measure of fictional closeness.
