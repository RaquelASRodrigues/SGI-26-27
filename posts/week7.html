Similar stories,
distant pages.

Are Marvel characters whose Wikipedia descriptions are similar also close in the hyperlink network—or do words and links tell different stories?

Two ways to be close.

A page can describe characters with similar powers, teams, or story arcs without linking to them. Conversely, a page can link to another character for a brief plot connection that leaves little shared language. Comparing text with links lets us see where these two kinds of relationship agree—and where the encyclopedia’s prose and its navigation diverge.

We use the 303 character pages supplied for this project and the Week 1 directed link network. Text neighbours are every other page whose cosine similarity meets the selected threshold. Hyperlink neighbours are pages joined to the selected character by a single direct edge; incoming links point to the selected page, outgoing links leave it. A multi-step path does not count as a hyperlink neighbour.

TF-IDF cosine. TF-IDF weights a page’s words by how frequent they are on that page and how uncommon they are across the corpus; cosine similarity compares the resulting vectors. It is fast and reproducible, but mostly captures shared vocabulary—not meaning expressed with different words. Stopwords are removed, term frequency is sublinear, and vectors are L2-normalized.
LSA cosine. We apply TruncatedSVD with 100 latent dimensions, 7 iterations and random seed 261021 to the same TF-IDF document-term matrix. The reduced page vectors are L2-normalized before cosine similarity. LSA can capture broader co-occurrence structure, but the chosen dimensionality and corpus shape its representation.
MiniLM embedding cosine. The locally run sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model encodes context into 384-dimensional vectors. We mean-pool its last-layer content-token representations and L2-normalize each page vector before cosine comparison. The default 256-token model input limit is handled by splitting every full page into non-overlapping chunks of at most 254 content tokens; all chunk token representations contribute, weighted by the number of content tokens. No page is silently truncated. This captures contextual similarities as well as shared wording, but chunk boundaries lose some long-range context.
mxbai embedding cosine. mixedbread-ai/mxbai-embed-large-v1 is enabled only when its pinned model output and validated local cosine matrix exist. In a suitably provisioned environment, install requirements-week7-mxbai.txt, then run python analysis/week7_mxbai_embeddings.py, python analysis/preprocess_week7_mxbai.py, and python analysis/week7_unexpected.py. The website consumes cached matrices only; it never downloads or runs a model. The embedding script records the model revision, uses its configured CLS pooling and L2 normalization, chunks every page within the model context limit, then computes a token-weighted normalized page vector.

All available methods use the same 303 full pages and node order. TF-IDF and LSA share the stop-word-filtered, sublinear unigram TF-IDF matrix; neural embeddings retain the full tokenizer stream and use the documented chunk-weighted aggregation. Each method has its own threshold. Scores have different scales and must not be compared numerically across methods.

A fixed-network null test

Loading the permutation test…

The null keeps the hyperlink graph and its directed edges fixed, then randomly reassigns the page text representations to nodes. Every run scores the same 1,784 directed edges. This asks whether the observed edge endpoints tend to carry more similar TF-IDF representations than random text-to-node assignment would produce. It does not show that shared wording caused links, control for page length, degree or editorial attention, or establish agreement for embedding semantics.

What the score can’t say.

TF-IDF is sensitive to repeated names, team labels, and formulaic article language. Cosine values depend on this corpus and preprocessing; a TF-IDF score should not be compared numerically with an embedding score from a different model. Page lengths and editorial coverage vary, and links reflect article conventions as well as character relationships. The text set also contains disconnected and isolated pages: they still receive text rankings, but have no direct hyperlink neighbours in the corresponding direction.

In this dataset, text similarity and direct links are related on average, yet they are not interchangeable. Some characters share nearly all of their top-ranked text neighbours with their direct links, while other high-ranking text pairs have no direct edge and some actual links connect pages with little shared vocabulary. The explorer makes those differences inspectable character by character; it does not turn either kind of similarity into a definitive measure of fictional closeness.

THE UNEXPECTED CONNECTIONS.

Semantically close. Structurally distant. A high text score paired with a long path—or no path at all—is a descriptive mismatch, not automatically a statistically surprising relationship. This module ranks actual pairs by similarity and separation, shows both directed travel distances, and compares a fixed top-similarity cohort with directed degree-preserving rewires. The null supports an aggregate test only; it does not assign significance to individual pairs.

An isolate has zero observed in-degree and out-degree (also degree zero in the undirected projection): it has no hyperlink path to other nodes, but its page can still be textually similar. This toggle filters the ranked pair population only; it never removes nodes from the network or the original explorer. Including isolates changes which pairs are ranked and may elevate textually similar but structurally disconnected pairs; it does not imply a directional bias.

Loading pair ranking…

Z-score is the observed pair’s path distance minus its mean finite distance across the degree-preserving null graphs, divided by the sample standard deviation. It uses the selected directed/undirected path definition; the text method and threshold only determine which pairs appear and do not enter the path statistic. Positive values are longer than the null expectation; negative values are shorter. It is conditional on finite null paths and descriptive, not a significance test. N/A means the observed pair is unreachable, or its null distribution has too few finite samples or zero variance. Ranking itself remains unchanged: unreachable pairs first, then longer observed paths, then higher text similarity. Aggregate empirical p-values and Holm corrections are reported separately.

What the null can—and cannot—tell us

Loading aggregate results across the most text-similar pairs…

The null uses 100 seeded directed target-swapped graphs (seed 721026), each with five accepted swaps per observed edge. It rejects self-loops and duplicate edges, fixes the 303-node set, and verifies every node’s in-degree and out-degree after each graph. It recalculates reachability and shortest paths separately for every randomized network. It does not preserve components, reachability, or shortest-path distances; short swap chains may not fully mix the constrained graph space. The aggregate cohort is the top 1% of eligible unordered pairs for each available text method and isolate setting; its finite mean path and unreachable fraction are compared with each randomized distribution, with Holm correction across all aggregate tests. Pair-specific null reachability frequencies are shown separately from conditional finite-distance z-scores; no pair-specific empirical p-values are implemented. These z-scores are descriptive and are not pairwise significance tests. A long path for one selected pair is not itself a significant result, and neither text similarity nor the rewiring comparison establishes causation.

What the strongest pairs reveal

Blue Eagle (character) and Moonglow (comics) score 0.252 by TF-IDF, with no directed route in either direction; the undirected projection connects them in three edges. The highest shared TF-IDF terms include squadron (0.05306 contribution) and overmind (0.02522). Both page excerpts identify the Squadron Supreme and describe the Overmind story, so their text match reflects a shared team/story context even though directed travel between their pages is impossible.

Backhand (character) and Blackthorn (character) score 0.259 by TF-IDF and have no direct edge, but the directed shortest path is three edges in both directions: Backhand → Vyking → Snapdragon (Morituri) → Blackthorn. Both pages explicitly identify the characters with Strikeforce: Morituri; morituri contributes 0.05709 to their TF-IDF cosine. This is a clear example of textual agreement with indirect, rather than direct, network connection.

Happy Sam Sawyer and Junior Juniper have no path even in the undirected projection. Their TF-IDF score is 0.188 and their embedding score is 0.621; both descriptions mention Sgt. Fury and the Howling Commandos, reflected by shared terms sgt, howling, commandos, and fury. The two scores come from different methods and are not numerically comparable. In the embedding results, Justice (New Universe) and The Witness (character) score 0.648, have no directed route, and are five edges apart in the undirected projection; shared contextual words such as paranormal are clues, not a word-level explanation of the embedding.
