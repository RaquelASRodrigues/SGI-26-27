Testing the Marvel Universe against Erdős–Rényi randomness, Barabási–Albert scale-free growth, Watts–Strogatz small worlds, and degree-preserving null models.

The Randomness Fallacy.

A CCDF asks a simple question: for degree k, what fraction of nodes have degree at least k? The directed curves separate incoming attention from outgoing editorial links, while the undirected curve collapses each character's neighbourhood into one degree. That distinction matters: popularity creates a heavy in-degree tail, while out-degree is shaped more by how much context an editor packs into an article.

The bands compare Marvel with repeated random draws. A tail that resembles a straight line on log–log axes cannot prove a power-law mechanism: finite-size effects, category curation, and a few hubs can create the same visual impression. We use the curve to compare models, not to claim that one plot reveals the network's origin story.

The Null Model Shuffle.

We performed 160 degree-preserving double-edge swaps. Every shuffled network keeps the observed degree sequence, but rewires which nodes meet. That makes the comparison sharper than ER: if Marvel's clustering remains high after accounting for each node's degree, local triangles are not merely an artefact of the degree distribution.

The histogram shows the clustering coefficient from every shuffle. The vertical line is the observed Marvel value; its distance from the null mean is summarized by the z-score and empirical p-value.

Model Scoreboard.

Erdős–Rényi · Random

ER preserves the number of nodes and edges but otherwise forgets who connects to whom. It is a useful baseline for path length and random clustering, yet its maximum degree is far too small and its model has essentially no isolated structure at this density.

Barabási–Albert · Preferential Attachment

BA grows by attaching new nodes to existing hubs, so it naturally produces a heavy tail and a much more plausible maximum degree. It still underestimates clustering and cannot reproduce the editorial directionality or the small islands outside Marvel's giant component.

Watts–Strogatz · Small World

Watts–Strogatz starts with local lattice structure and rewires it. The normalized curves show the trade-off directly: a little rewiring can shorten paths while preserving clustering, but too much rewiring destroys the local cohesion that defines a small world.

Growing Marvel vs Reality.

Preferential attachment gets the headline intuition right: hubs matter, and a growth model produces a much heavier degree tail than ER. But Marvel is also an edited, directed, culturally selective network. BA fails to reproduce the observed clustering, reciprocity, and isolated islands, so “the rich get richer” is only one ingredient in the network's history.
